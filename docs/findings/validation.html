This page reports the practical validation suite: 8 problems taken from embedded application domains, disjoint from both the search set and the held-out set, which no optimizer saw and no archive statistic includes. Discovered methods from the live archive and the classical anchors integrate each problem in Q15 at the same fixed cycle budget of 65536 cycles under m0plus_fast with floor rounding, scored as final-state error against an independent reference solution. The suite splits into 5 non-stiff practical problems and 3 moderately stiff ones, grouped separately below because stiffness changes which methods finish at all.

On the 5 non-stiff practical problems at a 65536-cycle budget under m0plus_fast with floor rounding, the best discovered method has lower Q15 error than the best classical anchor on 4 of 5; the median ratio of best-discovered to best-classical Q15 error is 0.475 (below 1.0 favors the discovered methods). On the 3 moderately stiff problems (stiffness ratios 292 to 1030): where both sides finish, the best discovered method is ahead on 1 of 2 with a median error ratio of 0.998; on 1 of 3 no discovered method finishes at all; every one overflows in Q15 while cheap low-stage classical methods complete the run. The pattern is the stability tax of explicit methods: at a fixed cycle budget an expensive high-order tableau takes larger steps, and once the step size times the fast eigenvalue leaves the stability interval the Q15 run overflows, whoever found the tableau. This is the motivating evidence for the epoch-3 implicit (SDIRK) track.

Measured wall clock agrees with the cycle model: in the benchmark head-to-head the champion tableau (11e898cb) runs in 36.293 us per Q15 step against 51.671 us for rk4 (medians across 7 problems, geometric-mean per-step speedup 1.450x); details on the benchmark page.

Problems

buck_converter

power electronics; oscillatory, 2 states, integrated to t = 25.

Non-stiff; stiffness ratio 1 (single complex pole pair; both eigenvalues share Re = -1/(2*Qf)).

x1' = D - x2; x2' = x1 - x2/Qf (D = 5/12, Qf = 2)

Reference: closed form via matrix exponential of the augmented affine system.

Source: Averaged CCM buck converter model, R. W. Erickson and D. Maksimovic, Fundamentals of Power Electronics, 2nd ed., Kluwer, 2001; the same averaged equations appear in arXiv:2002.02544. Per-unit normalization with Vg = 12 V, D = 5/12, L = 100 uH, C = 100 uF, R = 2 ohm (Z0 = 1 ohm, Qf = 2, omega0 = 1e4 rad/s). Startup transient from rest.

battery_2rc

battery management; linear, 3 states, integrated to t = 6.

Non-stiff; stiffness ratio 6.8 (tau2 / tau1 = 222.9 s / 32.8 s for the two RC branches).

dod' = T*I/(3600*Cn); vk' = T*(-vk/(Rk*Ck) + I/Ck), k = 1, 2

Reference: exact closed form: linear ramp plus two RC charge curves.

Source: Li-ion Thevenin 2RC equivalent circuit, M. Chen and G. A. Rincon-Mora, IEEE Trans. Energy Conversion 21(2):504-511, 2006, parameters for the 850 mAh TCL PL-383562 cell at SOC 0.5: R1 = 0.0467 ohm, C1 = 703 F, R2 = 0.0498 ohm, C2 = 4475 F. Constant 1.7 A (2C) discharge pulse is our scenario choice. States (dod, v1, v2), time unit 20 s.

bicycle_lateral

vehicle dynamics / ADAS; linear, 2 states, integrated to t = 15.

Non-stiff; stiffness ratio 1 (single complex pole pair (sideslip and yaw share their real part)).

v' = T*(-u*w + (Fy1+Fy2)/m); w' = T*(lf*Fy1 - lr*Fy2)/Iz; Fy1 = kf*((v+lf*w)/u - delta), Fy2 = kr*(v-lr*w)/u

Reference: closed form via matrix exponential of the augmented affine system.

Source: Linear-tire dynamic single-track model, Q. Ge, S. E. Li, Q. Sun, S. Zheng, arXiv:2011.09612 (2020), eqs. (1) and (3), model attributed to R. Rajamani, Vehicle Dynamics and Control, Springer 2011; Table I parameters (CarSim C-Class Hatchback 2017): m = 1412 kg, Iz = 1536.7 kg m^2, lf = 1.06 m, lr = 1.85 m, kf = -128916 N/rad, kr = -85944 N/rad. Constant 30 m/s, step steer 0.01 rad. States (v, w), time unit 0.1 s.

pll_lock

communications / clocking; nonlinear, 2 states, integrated to t = 10.

Non-stiff; stiffness ratio 1 (complex pole pair near the lock point (zeta = 0.7)).

theta_e' = w_free - Kvco*(x + (tau2/tau1)*sin(theta_e)); x' = sin(theta_e)/tau1

Reference: mpmath odefun at 30 digits (no closed form).

Source: Second-order type-2 analog PLL with active PI filter and sinusoidal phase detector, N. V. Kuznetsov, G. A. Leonov, M. V. Yuldashev, R. V. Yuldashev, arXiv:1705.05013, model (15). Kvco = 1, tau1 = 1, tau2 = 1.4 (wn = 1 rad/s, zeta = 0.7), frequency step w_free = 0.5 rad/s inside the lock-in range (no cycle slip). States (theta_e, x).

glucose_minimal

biomedical devices; nonlinear, 2 states, integrated to t = 12.

Non-stiff; stiffness ratio 1.5 (ratio of the two decay rates, 10*(0.01*y2 + p1) over 10*p2, at the y2 peak).

y1' = 10*(-0.01*y1*y2 + 0.0265*(0.82 - y1)); y2' = 10*(-0.0254*y2 + 0.1282*exp(-0.5*t))

Reference: mpmath odefun at 30 digits (no closed form).

Source: Bergman glucose minimal model (MINMOD) for an IVGTT, Pacini and Bergman, Comput. Methods Programs Biomed. 1986, via the MLAB worked example (civilized.com/mlabexamples/glucose.htmld/): p1 = 0.0265, p2 = 0.0254, p3 = 1.282e-5, Gb = 82 mg/dl, Ib = 8 uU/ml, G0 = 279 mg/dl. The insulin input is idealized as I(t) = Ib + 100*exp(-0.05*t_min) uU/ml (our modeling choice; the source drives it from measured data). Normalized y1 = G/100, y2 = 100*X, time unit 10 min. Only time-dependent RHS in the suite.

servo_load_step

motor control; linear, 2 states, integrated to t = 260.

Stiff; stiffness ratio 546.4 (electrical pole 9.649/ms over electromechanical pole 0.01766/ms).

di' = (-R*di - ke*dw)/L; dw' = (kt*di - dT_L)/J (deviations; per-ms coefficients -9.667, -9.75, 0.017476, -0.0074683)

Reference: closed form via matrix exponential of the augmented affine system.

Source: Armature-controlled DC servo, the standard two-state model (electrical and mechanical equations coupled by the back-EMF and torque constants; see e.g. Franklin, Powell and Emami-Naeini, Feedback Control of Dynamic Systems, ch. 2). Motor parameters from the maxon RE 25 catalog page, part 118752 (24 V, graphite brushes, 20 W): R = 2.32 ohm, L = 0.24 mH, kt = ke = 23.4 mNm/A, rotor inertia 10.3 g cm^2. Our scenario choice: a 12x reflected load inertia (total J = 13.39e-6 kg m^2) and a 10 mNm load-torque step from steady spin. States are deviations from the pre-step equilibrium, (delta_i / 1 A, delta_omega / 100 rad/s).

enzyme_qssa

biochemical kinetics; nonlinear, 2 states, integrated to t = 12.

Stiff; stiffness ratio 1030 (Jacobian eigenvalue ratio 128.4 over 0.1246 at t = 0; the separation narrows to about 130 as the substrate empties).

u' = -u + (u + 1/2)*v; v' = 64*(u - (u + 1)*v)

Reference: mpmath odefun at 30 digits (no closed form).

Source: Michaelis-Menten enzyme kinetics in the standard dimensionless form of the quasi-steady-state literature: Segel and Slemrod, SIAM Review 31(3):446-477, 1989; Murray, Mathematical Biology I, 3rd ed., Springer 2002, ch. 6. u is substrate, v the enzyme-substrate complex scaled by e0. Dimensionless parameters K = 1, lambda = 1/2, eps = e0/s0 = 1/64 are our scenario choice, placing the problem in the moderately stiff regime. The run starts on the QSS manifold v(0) = u(0)/(u(0)+K), the state reached after the fast initial burst.

robertson_scaled

chemical kinetics; nonlinear, 3 states, integrated to t = 200.

Stiff; stiffness ratio 292 (largest fast eigenvalue over the window (11.7, from the y2 equation) over the slow consumption rate a = 0.04; the third eigenvalue is 0 because y1 + y2/10 + y3 is conserved).

y1' = -a*y1 + (b/10)*y2*y3; y2' = 10*a*y1 - b*y2*y3 - (c/10)*y2^2; y3' = (c/100)*y2^2 (y2 stored 10x physical; y1 + y2/10 + y3 conserved)

Reference: mpmath odefun at 30 digits (no closed form).

Source: Reaction structure of the Robertson problem: H. H. Robertson, The solution of a set of reaction rate equations, in J. Walsh (ed.), Numerical Analysis: An Introduction, Academic Press, 1966, pp. 178-182 (original rates 0.04, 1e4, 3e7). Our scaling choices: rate constants cut to (a, b, c) = (0.04, 20, 250) and the intermediate stored as y2 = 10x its physical concentration, so the quasi-steady intermediate stays resolvable in Q15 while a clear fast/slow separation remains.
