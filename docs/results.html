How to read this

The x axis is the cycle number; the y axis is the running total of archive records, meaning every candidate that passed all nine verifier checks and was appended. Rejected candidates never appear here (they are counted in the rejection chart). Steep stretches are the exhaustive phases, where hundreds of enumerated points get verified per cycle; near-flat stretches are CMA-ES territory, where most sampled tableaus fail a check and the archive grows by survivors only.

How to read this

One bar per confidence tier. The tier is assigned by code the moment a record is appended, by comparison with the incumbent of its cell: heldout_verified means better than the incumbent on the search aggregate and the held-out aggregate, with improvement in at least two problem families; search_only means better on search but not on held-out, the signature of overfitting to the search set; unreplicated is everything else, including every record that lands in an empty cell. No model output touches the assignment, and the tier strings never appear in an LLM prompt.

How to read this

One bar per verifier rejection code, counted over the whole event stream. The nine checks run in a fixed cheap-to-expensive order and stop at the earliest failure, so each rejected candidate is counted once, under the check that killed it. The mix is dominated by the expensive tail — NO_ASYMPTOTIC_WINDOW (the measured-order fit found no consistent slope run) and Q15_OVERFLOW (the trajectory left the 2× amplitude margin) — because the cheap structural failures mostly cannot occur by construction: enumeration emits only valid points, and the CMA-ES projection solves b exactly so the order conditions already hold. A rejection has no side effects; it never enters the archive.

The rounding experiment

How to read this

Four groups, one per classical method. Within each group the blue bar is the error under floor rounding (what ARM's ASRS instruction does, and what the harness measures everywhere) and the orange bar is the same measurement rerun with round-to-nearest. Bar height is the RMS of final-state error over the three search-set problems, every method given the same 65,536-cycle budget under the fast multiplier, so a cheap method takes more, smaller steps. Compare the two bars within a group to see what rounding costs a method; compare blue bars across groups to see the ranking the harness actually reports.

Why it matters: floor injects a bias of half an LSB per multiply, always in the same direction, and at these step counts that bias accumulates faster than truncation error shrinks. That is how euler's blue bar ends up below rk4's. Round-to-nearest is a counterfactual measured outside the archive: the rounding mode is part of the pinned arithmetic, so it is compared here rather than searched over.

How to read this

Each row is one of the sixteen 2-stage order-2 tableaus whose coefficients are exactly representable (a21 = m/2s with s ≤ 6, and both b weights exact dyadics). a21 fixes everything else: the order conditions force b = (1 − 1/(2·a21), 1/(2·a21)) and c = (0, a21). "slow cycles" is the static per-step cost under the 32-cycle multiplier model, which is the sort key. "held-out error" is the archived end-to-end Q15 error on the held-out problems at equal cycle budget. The tier badge is the record's mechanically assigned confidence tier, and the last column names the point when it coincides with a textbook method. Click any row for the full archived score vector.

How to read this

One panel per classical method, from the day-one falsification run, all on the damped oscillator. Both axes are logarithmic: step size h on the x axis (smaller steps to the left), final-state error on the y axis. The blue line is Q15 fixed point; the orange line is float64 taking exactly the same steps, so the gap between them is pure arithmetic. Where the orange line keeps falling as h shrinks but the blue line flattens, roundoff has taken over from truncation; the dashed vertical marks that crossover. The project's premise lives in that flat region sitting inside the practical step-size range.
