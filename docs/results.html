At a fixed budget of 65,536 cycles on a Cortex-M0+ cost model, the search found Runge–Kutta methods that cut held-out error by up to 2.95× against the best classical method, and the data says why: Q15's floor rounding injects a −½-LSB bias per multiply that reorders the classical field before any search begins. The evidence below: an efficiency frontier where 13 of 14 discovered cells beat every classical anchor of equal or lower cost; a rounding-mode experiment in which Euler outranks rk4; the measured step size where quantization noise overtakes truncation error; a stiff problem where every classical method reports the reference norm as its error; one search space small enough to close with an exhaustive proof; and a validation suite of five practical problems from embedded applications, unseen by any search, where the discovered methods carry the lower error on four.

All numbers on this page share one setting unless a figure says otherwise: total budget 65,536 cycles, m0plus_fast cost model, Q15 fixed point with floor (ASRS) rounding, held-out problems. Cheaper methods therefore take more, smaller steps. Charts are built from tools/key_findings.json, recomputed from the archive; finding 6 reads rk-work/validation/results.json and uses its own practical problems.

1The efficiency frontier: discovered methods lead in 13 of 14 cells

The archive keeps one elite per MAP-Elites cell (order × stage count × cycle-cost bucket). At this snapshot the grid has 16 occupied cells: 14 held by discovered methods, 2 by classicals — euler owns the only order-1 cell, and rk38 still owns the 4-stage order-4 cell, where no discovered method displaced it. The fair comparison is against every classical anchor of equal or lower per-step cost, since a cheaper method could always be substituted; 13 of the 14 discovered cells win that comparison, with a median error ratio of 0.72 across the 14 discovered cells and a best of 0.34.

The one loss is the 17-cycle order-2 cell at 0.0902 against midpoint's 0.0844 (ratio 1.07). The best discovered method is a 3-stage order-2 tableau at 22 cycles per step, held-out error 0.0286 against 0.0844 for midpoint, the best classical anchor: A = [[0,0,0], [13/16,0,0], [5/32,11/32,0]], b = (0,0,1), c = (0,13/16,1/2). Every coefficient is an exact dyadic, so the method costs only shifts and adds; measured order 2.02. Per-problem it holds up across all four held-out problems: pendulum 0.0047, dc_motor 0.0277, rc_thermal 0.0461, quaternion 0.0191.

Three honest caveats. Cell elites are selected by held-out error among the archive's 45,000+ candidates, so champion values carry selection bias (the optimizer itself only ever sees the search-set error). The best cell's tier is "unreplicated"; tiers are mechanical insertion-time labels against the then-incumbent, not a validation grade, and 5 of the 14 discovered elites are heldout_verified. And classical held-out RMS is floored near 0.078 by the rc_thermal saturation (finding 4), which the discovered winners break; that floor flatters no one, but it is part of why the classical anchors bunch together.

2Floor rounding reorders the classical field

Every Q15 multiply here floors toward negative infinity, because that is what ARM's ASRS instruction does. Each floored product carries an expected bias of −½ LSB, always in the same direction. At these budgets that bias accumulates faster than truncation error shrinks, and it is large enough to reorder the classical methods. The counterfactual was measured directly: the same four methods, same budget, same problems, rerun with round-to-nearest.

On the three search-set problems, floor puts euler first (RMS 0.0129) and rk4 last (0.0590, 4.6× worse than euler); round-to-nearest restores the textbook order with rk4 ahead of euler (0.0289 vs 0.0469). Over all seven problems rk38 stays first in both modes, but floor compresses the field. Per problem, the best method changes on five of the seven outright; on dahlquist, floor puts three methods in an exact tie, and only dc_motor keeps the same winner cleanly.

Dahlquist is the clean mechanism case. Under floor, euler, heun2 and rk38 all report error 4.53999e-05, which is exp(−10), the entire reference value: the Q15 state decays to exactly zero, the true answer is below one LSB, and floor's downward bias lands on the right answer. Under round-to-nearest the state stalls in a dead zone above zero and the error is up to 2032× worse. The bias is not noise; it is a systematic force the winning methods exploit.

3Where quantization overtakes truncation

The project's premise was tested before the search started: if truncation error dominates roundoff at practical step sizes, classical analysis already gives the right answer and the search is pointless. The falsification run swept step size on the damped oscillator, computing each method's error twice: in Q15, and in float64 taking exactly the same steps, so the gap between the curves is pure arithmetic.

rk4's float error falls as h4 to 5.8e-11, while its Q15 error bottoms out at 1.13e-3 (h = 0.31), decouples from the float curve at h = 0.156, and sits at 2.58e-2 by h = 0.0098: below the crossover, more steps mean more floored multiplies, not more accuracy. heun2 decouples later, at h = 0.039. Both crossovers sit inside the practical step-size range, which is what the premise required.

The stored verdict is "mixed", and honestly so: coefficient arithmetic is 50–56% of per-step cycles under the fast multiplier (well above the 30% proceed threshold), but heun2 under the slow multiplier sits at 14.8%, a hair under the 15% kill line. The budget-level consequence is not mixed: at the shared budget under floor, rk4 wins 0 of 7 problems (euler and rk38 take three each, heun2 one); even under round-to-nearest it wins only quaternion, with rk38 taking five.

4The rc_thermal quantization floor

rc_thermal is the archive's stiff problem: a three-state RC network decaying toward equilibrium, derivative stored at DERIV_SCALE = 1/8, one LSB = 1.22e-4 in physical units. On it, all eight classical methods report errors between 0.1558 and 0.1582, a spread of 1.6%. That agreement is not convergence; it is collapse.

Each method drives the Q15 state to within 4–18 LSBs of the origin (euler ends at raw state (14,15,17), rk4 at (4,4,6)) while the true solution still has norm 0.1592 at t_end = 4. The reported error is essentially the reference itself. The mechanism is the floor bias again: late in the decay the scaled derivative terms are a few LSBs, floor turns them into extra decay, and the state falls to zero instead of tracking the slow mode.

Two measurements prove it is a floor artifact, not a Q15 limit. Under round-to-nearest, the same rk38 reaches 0.0511. And the best discovered method reaches 0.0461 while still using floor: the search found coefficients that keep the state out of the dead zone under the same arithmetic that kills every classical method. This is also why classical held-out aggregates are floored near 0.078 in finding 1.

5Phase 0, closed: an exhaustive result

Phase 0 is the one part of the space small enough to close completely: 2-stage methods with a21 on the dyadic lattice (|a21| ≤ 2, denominators to 26), 256 lattice candidates, of which exactly 16 have exactly representable b weights (the order conditions force b = (1 − 1/(2a21), 1/(2a21))). All 16 were enumerated, verified and archived; events.jsonl records the enumeration complete. This is a proof over the space, not a search result.

The optimum is a21 = −1/8 with b = (5, −4): held-out error 0.0613 at 15 cycles per step, a near-tie with a21 = −1/4 at 0.0614 (0.04% apart; call it a tie). Both are negative-a21 points no textbook lists, and both beat all eight classical anchors (best: midpoint at 0.0844). Within the family, midpoint ranks 7th and heun2 12th of 16. The pattern favours small negative a21: a probe step taken slightly backwards, with a large-coefficient correction, which under floor rounding partially cancels the accumulated downward bias.

6Practical validation: five problems nobody tuned for

Everything above is measured on the archive's own seven problems, so a fair question remains: do methods selected there transfer to dynamics nobody tuned for? The validation suite asks that directly, with five equations taken from real embedded application domains — an averaged buck converter (power electronics), a Li-ion 2RC battery equivalent circuit, a linear single-track vehicle model, a type-2 PLL acquiring lock, and the Bergman glucose minimal model — none of which any optimizer saw and none of which feeds any archive statistic. Discovered methods from the live archive and the classical anchors run each problem at the same 65,536-cycle budget in Q15 with floor rounding, scored against independent references: closed forms where the systems are linear, a high-precision integration elsewhere. The protocol is described under practical validation on the methodology page.

The discovered methods carry the lower error on four of the five problems, with a median best-discovered to best-classical error ratio of 0.475. The margin runs from decisive on the buck converter (0.00198 vs 0.0208 for rk38, a 10.5× gap) to moderate on glucose (ratio 0.556). On three of the four wins the winner is the same 3-stage order-2 champion from finding 1, coefficients unchanged; on the vehicle model an order-4 discovered method wins instead. The fifth problem is the honest miss: on the PLL, rk38 reaches 0.0128 against the champion's 0.0159 (ratio 1.24), so the transfer is strong, not universal.

Two checks keep the comparison clean. Float64 runs of the same tableaus at the same step counts land between 4e-14 and 8e-5 error, in every case hundreds of times below the Q15 number, so every error above measures quantization — exactly the regime the search selects for.
