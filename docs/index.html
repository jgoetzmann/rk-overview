rk — quantization-aware Runge-Kutta search

What the project is, why it exists, and what it has measured so far.

overviewarchitecturedesign decisionsresultsfindings snapshot (2026-08-30)live findings ↗

The rk project is an unattended, months-long search for explicit Runge–Kutta tableaus that minimise end-to-end error in Q15 fixed-point arithmetic at a fixed cycle budget on Cortex-M0+ — instead of the textbook criterion, truncation error in exact real arithmetic.

The falsifiable claim: classical tableaus were derived assuming exact arithmetic. On an FPU-less microcontroller in Q15, at step sizes a real controller would use, roundoff dominates truncation — so the classically optimal coefficients are not the practically optimal ones, and nobody has searched the gap between. Prior work (RKTK and its line) searches tableaus numerically for order and error constants; it does not price coefficients against a hardware cost model, and it does not evaluate in fixed point. That narrow gap is the project.

Three repositories with three trust levels do the work: rk-harness holds the verifier, evaluator and cost models and is mounted read-only into the run container; rk-work accumulates the append-only archive of every verified tableau; rk-findings receives an auto-generated site of numbers each cycle. This site, rk-overview, is the human layer on top.

Headline measurements

Everything below was measured by the harness; the findings snapshot carries the full tables.

  • The anchor result. rk4 and rk38 are both 4-stage order-4 methods with the same stability polynomial. Under a 1-cycle multiplier rk4 is cheaper (33 vs 36 cycles/step); under a 32-cycle multiplier rk38 is cheaper (64 vs 85). Which method to ship depends on which multiplier the chip shipped with — the thesis demonstrated in two textbook methods.
  • A proof, not a search, at order 2. The 2-stage order-2 family admits exactly 16 exactly-representable points (a21 = m/2^s, s ≤ 6). Exhaustive enumeration shows midpoint is the cheapest at 11 slow cycles — optimal within that space, with heun2 two cycles behind. A neighbouring point, a21 = -1/4 with b = (3, -2), has the better held-out error at 16 cycles.
  • Roundoff dominates where it matters. The day-one falsification experiment: rk4 spends 56% of its cycles on coefficient arithmetic under the fast multiplier (36% under the slow one), and its Q15 error stops improving below h ≈ 0.16 on the damped oscillator while float64 keeps falling — the roundoff/truncation crossover sits squarely in the practical range, so the project proceeds.
  • Floor rounding changes the answers. Q15 multiplies floor toward negative infinity (that is what ARM's ASRS does), and the resulting -½-LSB bias per term is the same order as the per-step dynamics at these budgets: at equal cycle budget Euler's search-set error undercuts rk4's, and the stiff rc_thermal problem collapses toward zero for every method. Swap floor for round-to-nearest and the ordering flips — see the rounding chart on the results page.
