Searching for Runge–Kutta methods that survive fixed-point arithmetic

An unattended search, a hash-pinned scorer, and what the numbers say so far.

An unattended search for explicit Runge–Kutta tableaus that minimise end-to-end error in Q15 fixed point at a fixed cycle budget on Cortex-M0+, not the textbook criterion of truncation error in exact arithmetic. Classical tableaus assume exact arithmetic; on an FPU-less microcontroller every multiply rounds, the rounding is biased, and nobody had searched the gap between classically optimal and practically optimal coefficients. The scored search covers explicit fixed-step methods; adaptive embedded pairs and implicit SDIRK methods exist as working prototypes with measured curves on the research tracks page. The whole thing runs inside a container that cannot edit its own scorer.

  • discoveryDiscovered integrators lead the classical anchors in 14 of 15 archive cells and on 4 of 5 practical embedded problems at an equal 65,536-cycle budget (best archive cell 2.95× lower error; practical-suite median ratio 0.475). key findings
  • speedThe champion integrates a step in 22 cycles to rk4's 33, and the measured wall clock agrees: 36.3 vs 51.7 µs/step (1.45× measured, r = 0.98 cycle model vs time), with the lower error on 5 of 7 problems. measured speed
  • infrastructureAn autonomous discovery harness (24/7 containerized search, 1,104 tests, a hash-pinned verifier, an LLM in the loop under guarded publication) found and mechanized the floor-rounding bias that reorders classical methods in 16-bit arithmetic. methodology
